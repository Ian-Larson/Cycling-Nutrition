

#### Highest Objective
- Help athletes plan nutrition for bike rides.

#### Current State Problems
- I have various bottles in my kitchen (550/750/950ml), various fueling and nutrition products with different nutrition macros: both solids and carb drink mix, and I have various rides that I do which vary in: duration (30 minutes -> 5 hour long ride), intensity (chill low intensity spin -> fast paced group rides), weather (sweat factor in the heat/summer is much higher), and carb targets (50/60/70+ grams of carbs/hour)

#### What I am looking For
- An app where I can input my ride characteristics, and will help me formulate a fueling plan for that ride.
- A plan for me would be:
	- What bottles to bring (My bike can only carry 2 bottles, but I may have many to choose from given what is clean on any given day in my pantry). Choose the smallest size combination that minimum meets my needs.
	- How many grams of mix to put into each bottle. I have a kitchen scale to measure this. Rounded scoop sizes might also be valuable.
	- Factor in solids. If I bring gels/chews, that will alter my plan for bottles. A total fuel plan will incorporate both of these.
	- A fueling guide. In simplest terms, tell me what I need to consume on the bike to meet my nutrition targets. I.e. tell me to eat 1 gel per hour and 1/2 of bottle 1...
	  
	  
#### Long term features
- Athlete profile
	- I should be able to input and save my FTP, size, weight, etc. then when planning a ride, I can input projected TSS for that ride. Using my power and Heart rate zones compared to the TSS, automatically give me a goal target for nutrition. For example: A high paced group ride/race that with a 200+ TSS might have a higher target carbs/hour than a 30 minute indoor recovery spin.
	  
	  
#### Guidelines
- Research modern, science backed nutrition planning for athletes & cyclists that has pushed nutrition consumption higher in recent years. This research should underpin any recommendation system.

#### UI and User Experience
- Great UX is at the center of everything you do. This tool should be easy to use, always clear, and attractive.
- Avoid using slop-like UI that most AI tools look like. This tool doesn't need to break ground visually though.
- Avoid using the most common fonts (ex: Inter, Roboto, etc.). Pick a beautiful, well readable font.
- The design needs to be native on both desktop browser and mobile.
  
  
  #### Coding Guidelines
- Commit early and often: With clear messages about what the commit solves.
- Break tasks into small issues and tackle them. Separate features, use best coding practices
